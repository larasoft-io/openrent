Who a change applies to

Each ruleset says whether it protects every existing tenancy immediately, only new contracts, or takes effect at the next renewal. Dashboards show the version in force for each tenancy.

Law

DXB-001LawAll tenancies

Written contract, registered in Ejari

Every tenancy must be in writing and registered with RERA through Ejari. Without Ejari you cannot connect DEWA, sponsor family or bring a case to the Rental Disputes Center.

How the platform enforces it: A tenancy cannot be marked active — and no rent is released — until the Ejari certificate number is on file.

Law 26/2007 Art. 4 (as amended by Law 33/2008)
DXB-006LawRenewals

Automatic renewal on the same terms

If the term ends and the tenant stays on without objection, the tenancy renews for the same period (or one year, whichever is shorter) on the same terms.

How the platform enforces it: If nobody acts before expiry, the platform prepares a like-for-like renewal and Ejari renewal.

Law 26/2007 Art. 6
DXB-009LawRenewals

Rent increases capped by the Smart Rental Index

On renewal, rent can only rise if it is more than 10% below the index rent for your building: up to 5% (11–20% below), 10% (21–30%), 15% (31–40%) or 20% (more than 40% below).

How the platform enforces it: The renewal form pulls the index band for the unit and blocks any figure above the permitted maximum.

Decree 43/2013 · DLD Smart Rental Index (2025)
DXB-014LawRenewals

90 days’ notice to change any term

Either side wanting to change the rent or any other term at renewal must tell the other at least 90 days before the contract ends, unless both agreed a different period.

How the platform enforces it: After the 90-day cut-off the “propose changes” action locks and renewal is offered on existing terms only.

Law 26/2007 Art. 14 (as amended)
DXB-016LawAll tenancies

Landlord keeps the home in good repair

Unless agreed otherwise, the landlord is responsible for maintenance and for fixing defects that affect the tenant’s use of the home.

How the platform enforces it: Major repairs are always routed to the landlord. Contracts cannot move structural, AC plant, plumbing or electrical faults to the tenant.

Law 26/2007 Art. 16
DXB-019LawAll tenancies

No alterations without consent

Tenants need the landlord’s permission (and any required permits) before making changes to the property.

How the platform enforces it: Alteration requests are logged and approved in-app so there is a record at move-out.

Law 26/2007 Art. 19
DXB-020LawAll tenancies

Security deposit is refundable

A landlord may take a deposit to cover damage, and must return it — or the balance — when the tenancy ends. The law sets no maximum; market custom is 5% (unfurnished) or 10% (furnished).

How the platform enforces it: See OR-101: openrent.me caps and safeguards deposits and sets a return deadline.

Law 26/2007 Art. 20
DXB-021LawAll tenancies

Hand back in the same condition, fair wear and tear excepted

Tenants return the home as they received it, allowing for normal use. Deductions need evidence.

How the platform enforces it: Timestamped move-in and move-out reports are compared side by side; deductions must reference a photo.

Law 26/2007 Art. 21
DXB-024LawAll tenancies

No subletting without written consent

A tenant cannot sublet or assign the tenancy unless the landlord agrees in writing.

How the platform enforces it: Sublet requests need a signed consent; see also DXB-S04 for rooms and bed spaces.

Law 26/2007 Art. 24
DXB-025ALawAll tenancies

Eviction during the term — limited grounds, 30-day notice

Before expiry a landlord can only seek eviction on listed grounds — for example unpaid rent 30 days after a notice served through a notary public or registered mail, unauthorised subletting, illegal use or serious damage.

How the platform enforces it: Arrears follow a fixed path: reminder → notarised 30-day notice → RDC file. The platform will not generate any other “eviction” letter.

Law 26/2007 Art. 25(1) (as amended)
DXB-025BLawAll tenancies

Eviction at expiry — 12 months’ notarised notice

To recover a home at the end of the term (owner’s or first-degree relative’s own use, sale, demolition or major works) the landlord must give at least 12 months’ notice, stating the reason, through a notary public or registered mail.

How the platform enforces it: Notices are drafted from the legal template, dated, and sent via the notary service; shorter periods and other reasons are rejected.

Law 26/2007 Art. 25(2) (as amended)
DXB-026LawAll tenancies

No re-letting after an own-use eviction

A home recovered for the owner’s own use cannot be let to someone else for 2 years (residential). If it is, the former tenant may claim compensation.

How the platform enforces it: The unit is locked from listing for 24 months after an own-use recovery.

Law 26/2007 Art. 26
DXB-027LawAll tenancies

A sale does not end the tenancy

If the property changes hands, the new owner takes over the existing tenancy on the same terms.

How the platform enforces it: Ownership transfers carry the tenancy, deposit record and payment schedule to the new owner’s account.

Law 26/2007 Art. 28
DXB-034LawAll tenancies

No cutting services or locking out

A landlord must not disconnect utilities or otherwise stop the tenant using the home. Disputes go to the Rental Disputes Center, not self-help.

How the platform enforces it: There is no such action in the product. Reports of a lock-out or cut-off are escalated to a manager within one hour.

Law 26/2007 Art. 34 · clarified March 2026
DXB-S04LawAll tenancies

Shared housing needs a permit — tenants cannot sublet rooms or bed spaces

Letting rooms, partitions or bed spaces is only allowed by the owner or an authorised operator holding a shared-housing permit, within occupancy and safety standards. Fines can reach AED 1,000,000.

How the platform enforces it: Listings are for whole units. Room-by-room letting is disabled unless a shared-housing permit is uploaded and verified.

Law 4/2026 on shared housing (in force since late Aug 2026)

Regulation and process

DXB-002RegulationAll listings

Advertising permit on every listing

A property may only be advertised with a valid Trakheesi permit issued for that unit, backed by a signed marketing agreement (Form A) with the owner. The permit number and QR code must appear on the advert.

How the platform enforces it: Listings cannot go live without Form A, a verified title deed and an active permit. Expired permits pause the advert automatically.

RERA advertising rules · Trakheesi permit & QR code
DXB-003RegulationAll tenancies

Only the owner (or a valid PoA) can let

The landlord on the contract must match the title deed, or hold a notarised power of attorney. Identity is confirmed with UAE Pass / Emirates ID.

How the platform enforces it: Title deed and ID are checked before listing, and again before contract signing.

DLD title-deed records · AML/KYC obligations for brokers
DXB-OCCRegulationAll tenancies

Everyone living in the home is registered

All occupants — family, flatmates, long-stay guests — should be recorded against the Ejari via Dubai REST.

How the platform enforces it: Tenants are prompted to add occupants at move-in and whenever the household changes.

DLD co-occupant registration (Dubai REST)
DXB-PAYRegulationNew tenancies

Payment terms are written into the contract

There is no mandatory number of cheques. Monthly, quarterly or half-yearly instalments are allowed when both sides agree, and must be stated in the contract. Rent is the same total whichever plan is chosen.

How the platform enforces it: The contract builder records the plan; changing instalments does not change the annual rent.

Unified tenancy contract · DLD Flexi Rent initiative (June 2026)
DXB-RDCRegulationAll tenancies

Disputes go to the Rental Disputes Center

The RDC hears all landlord–tenant disputes. The filing fee is 3.5% of annual rent (minimum AED 500, maximum AED 20,000); half is refunded if you settle.

How the platform enforces it: Mediation is offered first. If it fails, a complete case file (contract, Ejari, notices, ledger, photos) is exported.

Decree 26/2013 establishing the RDC

openrent.me standards

OR-101openrent.me standardNew tenancies

Deposits capped, safeguarded and returned in 14 days

Deposits are limited to 5% of annual rent (10% furnished), held in a safeguarded client account rather than by the landlord, and returned within 14 days of check-out unless a deduction is evidenced.

How the platform enforces it: Deposit field has a hard maximum. Release needs both parties, or a manager’s decision on the evidence.

OR-102openrent.me standardNew tenancies

A fair way out for tenants

Dubai law gives tenants no automatic right to leave early. Every openrent.me contract includes a break clause: 60 days’ notice and at most two months’ rent. Landlords can offer better, never worse.

How the platform enforces it: Break-clause sliders stop at the standard.

OR-103openrent.me standardNew tenancies

Small repairs threshold

Tenants cover minor fixes up to AED 500 per item (light bulbs, blocked drains they caused). Everything else is the landlord’s cost. The threshold can be lowered, not raised.

How the platform enforces it: Each maintenance job is tagged with who pays before a contractor is booked.

OR-104openrent.me standardAll tenancies

Emergency response within 4 hours

No cooling in summer, flooding, no power or a security risk are emergencies. If the landlord does not respond in 60 minutes, openrent.me dispatches an approved contractor up to a pre-agreed limit.

How the platform enforces it: Emergency tickets start a timer visible to tenant, landlord and manager.

OR-105openrent.me standardAll tenancies

No agency commission for tenants

Tenants never pay a percentage commission. Fees are flat, shown before you commit, and government fees are passed on at cost.

How the platform enforces it: Fee table is the only source of tenant charges.
